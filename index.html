Your relationship to the model

To you the model is an artifact you shape: data, architecture, training objective, fine-tuning, and evaluation against the behavior you want. You change what the model knows and how it behaves, and you are the one who can say why it got better or worse.

This org covers ML engineering — building and training the models: data pipelines, classical and deep learning, fine-tuning and alignment, evaluation, distributed training, and the senior-to-principal work of setting ML architecture and strategy.

What you own

  • Training data: sourcing, curation, labeling, and quality
  • Training and fine-tuning: objectives, recipes, and experiments
  • Model-level evaluation: benchmarks, regressions, and comparisons
  • Model artifacts: versions, model cards, and lineage
  • Choosing between training, fine-tuning, distilling, or using a vendor model as-is

What you don't own: The serving fleet, the product features built on top, or the final call on whether a use is acceptable. You hand off a model with evidence of what it can and can't do.

Who you work with

No curriculum owns the model end to end. These are the handoffs with each of the others.

  • AI Workforce

    Work alongside the models

    You hand them
    Models that are better at real work, plus honest documentation of what they're bad at
    They hand you
    Concrete examples of where model quality falls short on real tasks, the raw material for better evals and training data

  • AI Startup

    Turn it into a company

    You hand them
    An honest read on what's technically feasible, how long it takes, and what it costs to own a model versus renting one
    They hand you
    The problems worth owning a model for, and the business case for the data and compute it takes

  • AI Governance

    Govern, secure, and assure AI

    You hand them
    Data provenance, model cards, training-data documentation, and evaluation results they can audit
    They hand you
    Data-use rules, documentation standards, and the risk-based evaluation bar a model must clear

  • AI Engineering

    Build with the models

    You hand them
    Models, fine-tunes, and embeddings with documented strengths, failure modes, and benchmark results on relevant tasks
    They hand you
    Real failure cases and task data from production, which tell them where a fine-tune or a different model would actually help

  • AI Infrastructure

    Run the platforms models live on

    You hand them
    Model artifacts with known resource profiles (memory, throughput, precision) and the training workloads they need to schedule
    They hand you
    Training and fine-tuning clusters, experiment and artifact storage, and a path to production for the models they produce

How the tracks work

Learn

Each track is a sequence of modules: lecture chapters, hands-on exercises, quizzes, and projects. Start with Module 00 and work forward.

Check your work

The paired solutions repo has a reference walkthrough for every exercise. Attempt each one first; the gap between your answer and the reference is where the learning happens.

Built in the open

The content is written with AI assistance and under ongoing human review, so it can contain mistakes. Report anything wrong through the repo's Issues; fixes ship continuously.
